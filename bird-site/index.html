Bird Migration & Highway Noise

Interactive map showing how traffic sounds impact avian migration patterns

Our Solution

This interactive map visualizes the relationship between highway noise and bird migration patterns by combining bird observation data with highway noise projections.

Our analysis focuses on the I-95 corridor and its impact on local bird populations. By integrating data from eBird observations with FHWA traffic noise models, we've created a comprehensive view of how transportation infrastructure affects avian behavior and migration routes.

How to Use the Map

Click on any circle marker to see detailed information about bird observations and noise levels at that location.

Study Area

The highlighted polygon represents a 5-mile buffer zone along the I-95 highway corridor, our primary area of analysis.

How to Use the Map Legend

Select different projection levels to see how changing traffic volumes would affect bird observations along the I-95 corridor. The color gradient on the map shifts from green (quieter) to red (louder) based on your selection. The size of the circle reflects the number of birds observed at the station.

Understanding the Noise Projections

What Do the Projection Percentages Mean?

Each monitoring station along I-95 has its own baseline noise level. The projection percentages (-30% to +30%) represent relative changes in traffic volume that affect all locations proportionally.

Quieter Scenarios
-30% to -10%

Reduced traffic, improved bird habitat

Current Baseline
0%

2023 traffic conditions

Busier Scenarios
+10% to +30%

Increased traffic, more bird disruption

Example

Typical Impact: A +30% traffic increase adds ~1-2 dB. For context: 65 dB = busy restaurant, 70 dB = vacuum cleaner, 75 dB = heavy traffic, 80 dB = garbage disposal. Even small dB increases significantly impact bird communication ranges.
